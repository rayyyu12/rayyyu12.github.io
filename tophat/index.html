Tophat

Automation for a futures trading strategy, run across a fleet of prop‑firm accounts from the first evaluation to the final payout.

Python · FastAPI · Monte Carlo

Sold · Under NDA

Sold, and under NDA. Tophat was bought in 2026 for $50,000 up front, and I stay on as its paid developer and maintainer. Its code, dashboard and strategy details are confidential, so there's no demo or source to show, but this page covers what it does at a high level.

What it does

Tophat runs one futures strategy many times over, across a fleet of prop‑firm accounts. Each morning it decides what every account should trade, places the orders, and reconciles the results.

The hard part is everything around the trade. Prop-firm accounts move through a lifecycle of evaluation, funding, payout cycles and retirement, each under a trailing drawdown floor and rules that differ from firm to firm. Tophat tracks where every account sits in that lifecycle and decides what each one should trade today, or whether it should sit out.

Where the edge comes from

Most automated strategies built by retail traders have no real edge, and on its own, this one doesn't either. The edge is in how prop firms work: an account can never lose more than it cost to get, while a funded account can pay out many times that. Across enough accounts, that lopsided payoff is what makes it profitable. Tophat exists to play that game at scale and by the rules.

Working with Claude

I built Tophat with Claude as a working partner, and it wouldn't exist without it. Writing the code was the smaller part. The bigger one was the research: Claude ran the backtests, Monte Carlo simulations and statistical analyses that turned a rough idea into a fine-tuned set of parameters, then helped apply them to the live system. Together we worked out:

  • which firms and account types suit the strategy
  • account size, position sizing and profit targets
  • when to trade, and how to spread risk so the accounts don't all ride on one trade
  • the expected value of each account, and how often to add new ones

How it works

  • One engine, many firms. Accounts at firms with an API are traded directly. Firms without one are handled too: Tophat models those accounts from the ones it trades, applying each firm's own rules.
  • A bridge that checks its own work. A companion service keeps the outside tools Tophat relies on in sync every night. It backs up before each change, verifies the result, and rolls back and reports if anything looks wrong.
  • Simulation on the live code. Backtests and Monte Carlo runs replay historical tick data through the same engine that trades live, so the simulator and the real system can't drift apart. The same seed always gives the same result.
  • Risk built into the scheduler. Daily caps on the riskiest trades, staggered entries so the fleet never moves at once, a guard that skips any account with an open position, and one attempt per account per day.
