Throttle Tune

A real-time engine sound simulator for an electric scooter. Twist the throttle and it behaves like an M4, a Supra, a Hellcat, or a Lamborghini SVJ, with the turn-signal buttons as paddle shifters.

C++17 · SDL2 · Raspberry Pi · Real-time audio

Source on GitHub

What it does

Electric scooters are silent. Throttle Tune reads the throttle signal through an ADC on a Raspberry Pi and plays a convincing engine sound back in real time, with idle, revs, pulls and deceleration burbles, all controlled by your hand. The turn-signal buttons double as paddle shifters, so you can run up through the gears and drop back down yourself.

Each car is its own model of an engine, not just a different set of samples. The M4 has launch control and RPM-staged revs. The Supra smooths the throttle and picks clips by how hard you're pushing, from a light cruise up to a violent pull. The Hellcat runs a virtual engine with RPM physics, a five-speed gearbox, rev-matched downshifts and a layered mix of rumble, supercharger whine and exhaust. The SVJ is a six-speed you shift yourself on the turn-signal buttons, with an ignition cut and an exhaust pop on most upshifts and backfires on downshifts. When it's stopped, the same buttons arm launch control or drop it into neutral to rev in place.

How it works

  • One job per thread. An ADC thread reads the throttle and publishes it through a std::atomic, and the buttons are interrupt-driven. The audio loop runs under SCHED_FIFO on its own pinned core and doesn't wait on a lock.
  • Look it up. Gear multipliers, engine braking and downshift thresholds live in constexpr tables, and throttle history sits in a fixed-size ring buffer.
  • Layered, not looped. Volumes crossfade on equal-power curves, so moving between sound layers is heard as the engine changing, not as a jagged clip switch.
  • Ported from a prototype. It started as a 3,000‑line Python script, jumper wires, a breadboard, and a potentiometer. It now has a single PCB and a Raspberry Pi. The C++ rewrite splits it into about twenty files, wraps every SDL resource in RAII, and loads samples in parallel at startup.
